**Prose Rules**

**Core Style**
- Cut filler, generic transitions, and moral wrap-ups.
- Use strong, active Anglo-Saxon verbs. Drop -ly adverbs and Latinate buzzwords.
- Minimize adjectives and adverbs.
- Vary sentence openings and structure. Mix short sentences, fragments, and longer ones.
- End-weight: put the strongest word last.
- Compress preposition chains into possessives or direct verbs.
- Ban clichés and dead metaphors. Stay literal or invent fresh imagery.
- Write with an authoritative, slightly irreverent voice that prioritizes clarity and bite.

**Advanced Vocabulary**
- Asymmetry: when pairing modifiers, avoid predictable pairings; create tension or unexpected contrast.
- Preposition purge: rewrite chains of “of,” “for,” and “to” using possessives, direct adjectives, or stronger verbs.
- Gerund grind ban: minimize progressive -ing forms; convert to simple past or present.

**Structure & Cadence**
- Periodic sentences: occasionally withhold the main verb or core point until the end of a long sentence.
- Echo check: do not repeat a distinct noun, verb, or adjective within the same paragraph unless used intentionally as a device.
- Front-loading: begin sentences with the true subject or a compelling action; avoid “There are,” “It appears that,” or similar weak openers.
- End-weight (reinforced): conclude sentences on the most important, punchy, or surprising word.
- Structural variety: do not start consecutive sentences with the same grammatical pattern.
- Loose punctuation: use em-dashes for sharp afterthoughts or shifts; use semicolons to link two closely related weighty ideas without a conjunction.
- Monosyllabic slam: deliver key conclusions or transitions in sentences made entirely of short, single-syllable words.
- Polysyndeton: for urgency or momentum, repeat coordinating conjunctions instead of standard comma separation.

**Equations & Technical Terms**
- Keep symbols inline only when they are the grammatical object or the final weighted term; otherwise name the object and let the symbol follow after a colon or em-dash.
