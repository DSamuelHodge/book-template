\chapter{The Many Faces of Softmax}\label{ch:softmax}

\newthought{Softmax looks like} a rule for turning scores into weights. That
description is correct and thin. The same map is the gradient of a convex
function, the moment map of a torus action, and the inverse gradient of
negative entropy, up to a common shift in the logits. Each face shows what
the others hide.

\section{Scores modulo a common shift}

Let $z\in\mathbb{R}^N$ be a vector of logits. The \defn{softmax} map sends
those scores to a probability vector:
\begin{equation}
  p_i(z) = \frac{e^{z_i}}{\sum_{j=1}^N e^{z_j}},
  \qquad i=1,\ldots,N.
  \label{eq:softmax}
\end{equation}
The denominator forces the entries to sum to one. More quietly, softmax
throws away one degree of freedom. For any scalar $c$,
\begin{equation}
  p(z+c\mathbf{1})=p(z).
  \label{eq:softmax-shift}
\end{equation}
Only score differences matter; a common offset has no vote.

Thus softmax does not map all of $\mathbb{R}^N$ injectively to the simplex.
It maps logits modulo constant shifts to the interior of the probability
simplex. Every strictly positive probability vector has a representative:
$z_i=\log p_i$ works, and every other representative adds the same constant
to all entries. Boundary probabilities require a limit, as one or more logit
differences run off to infinity.

\section{The gradient face}

The first face appears in the \defn{log-sum-exp} function,
\begin{equation}
  A(z)=\log\sum_{i=1}^N e^{z_i}.
  \label{eq:log-sum-exp}
\end{equation}
Differentiate once and the scores become probabilities:
\begin{equation}
  \nabla A(z)=p(z).
  \label{eq:softmax-gradient}
\end{equation}
Softmax is not an extra trick bolted onto a partition function. It is the
partition function's slope.

Differentiate again. The Hessian is
\begin{equation}
  \nabla^2 A(z)=\operatorname{diag}(p)-pp^\mathsf{T}.
  \label{eq:lse-hessian}
\end{equation}
This matrix is the covariance matrix of a categorical outcome with
probabilities $p$. It is positive semidefinite, and its null direction is
$\mathbf{1}$: shifting every logit together changes nothing. The Hessian sees
relative scores and ignores the dead common mode.

\section{The entropy face}

The \defn{Legendre dual} of $A$ is its convex conjugate: maximize
$z\cdot p-A(z)$ over logits. The result is negative Shannon entropy on the
simplex,
\begin{equation}
  A^*(p)=
  \begin{cases}
    \displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^N p_i\log p_i,
      & p_i\geq 0,\ \sum_i p_i=1,\\
    +\infty, & \text{otherwise},
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:lse-conjugate}
\end{equation}
with $0\log 0=0$ at the boundary. This sign matters: Shannon entropy is
$-\sum_i p_i\log p_i$; the convex dual carries the minus sign.

The link is not merely a match of formulas. At $p=\nabla A(z)$, the
Fenchel equality reads
\begin{equation}
  A(z)+A^*(p)=z\cdot p.
  \label{eq:fenchel-equality}
\end{equation}
Indeed, $\log p_i=z_i-A(z)$; multiply by $p_i$, sum, and use
$\sum_i p_i=1$. The logit vector and the probability vector are dual
coordinates, with their pairing balanced by log-sum-exp and negative
entropy.

This duality also explains the shift symmetry. Adding $c\mathbf{1}$ to $z$
adds $c$ to $A(z)$ and to $z\cdot p$; their difference stays fixed because
$p$ has unit mass. The conjugate cares about the distribution, not which
representative of its logits produced it.

\section{The moment-map face}

The third face lives in complex projective space. Let
$[w_1:\cdots:w_N]\in\mathbb{CP}^{N-1}$. The torus $T^{N-1}$ rotates the
coordinate phases, modulo the common phase that projective space already
forgets. With the standard normalization, its \defn{moment map} has components
\begin{equation}
  \mu_i([w])=\frac{|w_i|^2}{\sum_{j=1}^N |w_j|^2},
  \qquad i=1,\ldots,N.
  \label{eq:projective-moment-map}
\end{equation}
The image is the closed probability simplex. Points with every coordinate
nonzero map to its interior; zero coordinates land on its boundary.
\citeneeded

Now set $w_i=e^{z_i/2}$. Equation~\ref{eq:projective-moment-map} becomes
exactly Equation~\ref{eq:softmax}. The logits set coordinate magnitudes;
the torus rotates phases; the moment map keeps the normalized squared
magnitudes and drops those phases. The common logit shift rescales every
$w_i$ by the same factor, which projective space discards.

\section{One map, three jobs}

The gradient view says how probabilities respond to logits. The dual view
says which convex potential belongs to those probabilities. The moment-map
view says how phase symmetry collapses to a simplex. None replaces the
others; each names a different structure carried by the same coordinates.

In attention, the query-key scores enter as logits, and softmax turns them
into the weights that mix values. The map keeps relative score information
as a distribution. It discards the common offset, and in the projective
picture it discards phase. That is not a defect to patch. It is the
reduction that makes scores act as probabilities.
